Research Interests

Genomics and bioinformatics applied to the study of evolution and conservation

Population Genomics

In species that are elusive or difficult to study, I use population genomic datasets to uncover patterns gene flow and population dynamics. I have applied this concept to a range of species including the western chicken turtle, Pacific gray whale, and Montezuma quail. These projects can provide important context for conservationists managing populations or species of concern.

Comparative Genomics

Just as I am interested in fine-scale diversity at the population or species level, I am also fascinated by the factors driving diversity across evolutionary time. I use comparative genomics to compare divergence within genera of different taxonomic groups, and identify factors such as life history traits that may be correlated with increased divergence.

Pangenomics

I am excited about the growing resources for pangenomics in vertebrates and non-model organisms. To increase accessibility to pangenomes for projects with limited funding, I helped develop and test a short-read pangenome assembly pipeline that captures a high proportion of variants found in long-read assemblies.
